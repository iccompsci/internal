Dynamic Electric Field, Interactive (DEFI) Yemi Afolabi ’ 20 Computer Science; Professor John Barr, Computer Science In association with: Liana Rodell ’20 Physics; Collen Countryman Professor Physics Summer 2019-Fall 2019 Purpose: DEFI is an app that’s a collaboration between Ithaca College’s Physics and Computer Science Department. Our main goal was creating an app that can dynamically visualize the electric field interactions between protons and electrons to help students better understand what electric field interactions look like.

Background: A proton is a stable subatomic particle occurring in all atomic nuclei, with a positive electric charge. An electron is a stable subatomic particle with a charge of negative electricity. An electric field is a region around a charged particle or object within which a force would be exerted on other charged particles or objects.

Method: We used React Native to program the app . It uses a retouched version the programming language JavaScript. It can run both In Android and iOs simultaneously. We also used a graphics library called SVG (Scalable Vector Graphics) to create our shapes and lines. To calculate our electric field we utilized a vector library that had functions to do add, subtract, and, multiply, among other to create a resultant vector. We then scaled down the vector to fit the pixels of a mobile device. To get our lines we calculated the vectors from the initial position of the particles then continued to build from the position of the ending of the previous vector then we rendered the graphics for the resulting lines based upon the calculations. As the charge changes we have to recalculate and then re-render the graphics
