Deconstructing IronMarch: Spatial Analysis of Online Fascism Mateo Hernandez & Will Regalado Succop

Ithaca College Computer Science Motivation

Spatial Analysis

Methodology

Iron March was a far-right, neo-Nazi online messaging board. Iron March members have been arrested in the United States and Europe for murder, violent assault, possession of illegal firearms and explosives, and conspiracy to commit mass shooting. In 2019, the database hosting all Iron March content was leaked; this leak included the IP addresses of all members. Inspired by a spatial inquiry into membership of German farright group PEGIDA, we realized a spatial analysis of Iron March membership, extrapolated from member IP addresses, could illuminate potential catalysts of far-right extremism.

Future Research The identification and prevention of far-right extremism is a constant endeavor, and there is always more work to be done. Our inquiry was done on a relatively small dataset, concerning only a single online meeting place for fascist, while there are many. Furthermore, the spatial analysis we did to the data was almost entirely manual, and this would not be practical for a larger set of data. Thus, our future research would involve the acquisition of larger sets of data, as well as the automation of IP geolocation and mapping. Finally, following a large-scale inquiry into online fascism, our data would be presented to and analyzed by sociologists and political theorists more equipped to interpret our data.

Our analysis provided some surprising results. Both active and inactive members were focused on the eastern side of the country, with the majority in the north-east. Moreover, members were overwhelmingly located in Democratic leaning counties. There are certainly questions to be asked based on our findings. Why are members focused on the east coast? Why are the members on the west coast less active? Why are members mostly located in Democratic leaning locations? There are a large concentration of silent dots in Massachusetts, as well as a few other places. Why is it that some locations are densely populated by Iron March members, and in parts of the country where they are active, and are still mostly quiet?
