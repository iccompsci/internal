Time Awareness and Management Evaluation Kaiden Jones, Class of 2025; Toby Dragon, Faculty advisor

Introduction ADHD is a disorder that has seen a signiﬁcant increase in prevalence, particularly amongst adolescents (8-12). Understanding ADHD’s individual symptoms enables children and their guardians to manage the disorder eﬀectively rather than eliminate it. The objective of this project is to develop a program that observes and assesses time blindness, a common symptom of ADHD, in adolescent children. The program aims to evaluate how each child perceives time while engaged in tasks and provide children and their guardians with an individualized evaluation. This evaluation can help compensate for time blindness in daily life.

Project Utilizing the Godot software to develop 2D games and a user interface, users engage in four separate trials for predetermined durations. Each trial tests a speciﬁc aspect of time blindness, as detailed in the methods section. Each type of trial compares two times: a predetermined amount of time and an estimated/actual time. By comparing the predetermined times to the users' estimated/actual times, the assessment evaluates the accuracy of their time perception.

Methods

Methods

Retrospective Time Estimation: Process of estimating the duration of a task after the task is complete, while being aware that they would need to recall the duration of the trial.

Prospective Memory Processing: Process of forming a representation of a future action, temporarily storing that representation in memory, and retrieving it at a future time point Task Time Estimation Accuracy: Process of estimating the duration of a task before the task is complete.
