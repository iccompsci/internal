Analyzing Colonialism through the lenses of a Database Nardos Mamo and Simret Melak Ithaca college, Computer Science Department

Introduction During European colonization in Africa, arbitrary partitioning, resource exploitation, cultural assimilation, political domination, divide-and-rule policies, and the legacy of violence caused by colonization contributed to postcolonial wars in Africa. These actions disrupted local societies, created economic disparities, eroded cultural identities, and sowed seeds of division, leading to social tensions, conflicts, and instability that persist to this day.

Relational Database We used relational database to show the effects of the conflict on the economy of the colonies because • Data Integrity: Relational databases provide strong support for data integrity, allowing you to ensure the accuracy and consistency of the data. This helps ensure that the data accurately reflects the state of the economy during the time of the conflict. • Analytical Capabilities: Relational databases often come with built-in support for analytical functions and aggregations, which can be valuable for analyzing the economic data.

Our database project aims to examine the lasting impact of colonialism in Africa, using both relational and graph databases. We explore major conflicts in Africa since decolonization and trace their roots to events that occurred during the colonial era. Through this, we demonstrate how these conflicts have affected the continent's economy, resources, and geopolitical relations, both historically and in the present day. Furthermore, we highlight the relationships between each conflict, the involved countries, and international organizations, and the reasons behind their involvement.

We can know how much a specific conflict affected a country’s economy by joining the African_colonies and the economy table and entering the year the conflict happened (we can refer the year from the conflict table)

Future Research (GIS Systems) We can use GIS to map out the distribution of natural resources, such as oil or minerals, in relation to colonial boundaries or conflicts. • GIS allows us to integrate various types of data with geographic data, enabling us to explore complex relationships between different variables in a spatial context. • GIS can visually represent complex data sets, allowing us to more easily identify patterns and trends. • GIS can help us understand the spatial distribution of phenomena, such as natural resources or land use patterns, in relation to colonial boundaries or conflicts.

Methodology In our project, we utilized both graph and relational databases and found that each had its own level of convenience in different scenarios. For example, graph databases were more suitable for showcasing the relationships between different countries involved in a conflict and the connections between different conflicts. On the other hand, relational databases were more convenient for depicting the effect of the conflict on the economy of the colony. By leveraging the strengths of both graph and relational databases, we were able to effectively model and analyze different aspects of our data in a comprehensive and efficient manner, providing valuable insights.

Graph Database We used graph database to show the relationship between the different countries and organizations involved in the conflict because • Graph databases are designed to handle complex relationships between data entities, making them well-suited for scenarios where relationships are a key focus • Graph databases excel at handling connected data and are highly scalable horizontally. They are optimized for traversing relationships and can efficiently handle queries that involve complex, multi-level relationships without sacrificing performance.
