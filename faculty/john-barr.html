John Barr

Professor Barr's research interests currently span two areas: mobile technology and computer systems. Contemporary culture has become increasingly centered around smartphones and tablets, and much of the computing across various fields now takes place on these devices. Professor Barr's research explores the use of mobile technology broadly, with a particular emphasis on applications that serve the public good and support education.

There are currently several opportunities for students to participate in developing mobile applications across a range of disciplines. These include creating a cross-platform app that simulates electromagnetic fields, working alongside physics students to develop animations for iOS and Android devices; collaborating with physical therapy students on an app to monitor athletes' nutritional needs; continuing the development of an accessibility app that maps accessible routes across the Ithaca College campus; and developing an app that helps students address food insecurity.

In addition, Professor Barr is exploring the use of field-programmable gate arrays (FPGAs) in the classroom. FPGAs are hardware devices that can be reconfigured using specialized hardware description languages. These languages allow us to design processors, memory, and specialized hardware for applications such as machine learning. Students involved in this project would explore hardware design languages and develop instructional materials for incorporating FPGAs into various computer systems courses.
