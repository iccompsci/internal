Data Science Major

Our B.S. degree in Data Science is offered in collaboration with the Mathematics department of Ithaca College. Please refer to the overview in the Mathematics Department catalog page.

Working with Data

Data scientists begin with questions: What can we learn from the evidence, and what might the evidence leave out? Students practice finding, cleaning, organizing, and analyzing information, then explaining their conclusions to others. They learn to recognize uncertainty, work collaboratively, and make responsible choices about the use of data.

The goal is not simply mastery of today's tools. It is learning to think with data: to ask useful questions, judge the strength of an analysis, and communicate what the results do and do not show.

An Interdisciplinary Education

The major brings together mathematics, statistics, computer science, and knowledge of a field in which data is used. Jointly offered by the Mathematics and Computer Science departments, it includes foundations in both subjects, courses focused specifically on data, and courses in an application area students choose. Faculty from different disciplines help students see how these approaches inform one another.

Domain knowledge matters because data never exists apart from a context. Understanding what a measurement represents, how it was collected, and why a question matters can be as important as selecting an analytical method.

The Senior Project

Students have an opportunity to select or propose a substantial question for a senior project that brings their skills together. Faculty in mathematics and computing guide the analytical work, with input from a faculty member familiar with the project's subject. The project provides a chance to explore an individual interest and demonstrate the ability to carry an investigation from a question to a clear account of its findings.

Data Science in Practice

Data science can intersect with health and epidemiology, biology and bioinformatics, journalism, marketing, business, and many other fields. For example, the questions behind an analysis of health outcomes differ from those behind a data-driven news story, even when some of the same computational techniques are used.

Ithaca College's Data Talks series offers another window into these connections. Past presentations have drawn on work in biology, sociology, marketing, exercise science, and other disciplines, exploring topics from music classification to coral reefs. Such encounters show the range of questions that data can help illuminate.

Data Science and AI

Both majors use programming, mathematics, and data, but they emphasize different questions. Data Science concentrates on investigating evidence, finding patterns, and explaining what the findings mean. Artificial Intelligence concentrates on building, training, and evaluating systems that learn from data or perform tasks associated with intelligence. Students interested in both will find important connections between them.
